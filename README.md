# Probability: interactive animations

Interactive animations for an undergraduate probability course.
Each animation is a standalone web page built with [Plotly](https://plotly.com/javascript/).

**Open them at <https://marcevrard.github.io/proba-animations/>.**

## Animations

- **[PDF and CDF](https://marcevrard.github.io/proba-animations/pdf-cdf/pdf-cdf.html)**

  This page shows that $F_X(x) = P(X \le x)$ is the area under the PDF up to $x$ and the height of the CDF at $x$.
  You can drag $x$ along the axis of the PDF or of the CDF, or drag a probability along the axis of the CDF.
  A toggle switches to an interval, $P(x_1 < X \le x_2) = F_X(x_2) - F_X(x_1)$.
  $X$ follows a normal, logistic, exponential, or uniform distribution, and sliders set its parameters.
  The page shows its PDF, CDF, and quantile function.
  A zoom on $x$ helps with narrow densities.
  Add `#norm`, `#logis`, `#expon`, or `#unif` to the URL to choose the starting distribution.

- **[Universality of the uniform](https://marcevrard.github.io/proba-animations/uniform-universality/universality.html)**

  This animation shows both directions of the theorem, $X = F^{-1}(U)$ and $F(X) \sim \mathrm{Unif}(0, 1)$.
  Quantiles of $U$ are projected through the quantile function of $X$ and gather where its density is high.
  In the other direction, the same points are projected through the CDF and spread evenly over $(0, 1)$.
  You can choose the distribution of $X$: $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, $\mathrm{Logistic}(0, 1)$, $\mathrm{Exp}(1)$, or $\mathrm{Unif}(-2, 2)$.
  You can also choose the number of quantiles, the mode (all at once or random order), and the unit.
  The curve is dashed outside the support of $X$, where the CDF is flat at 0 or 1.
  Add `#quantile` or `#cdf` to the URL to choose the starting direction.

- **[Memoryless property](https://marcevrard.github.io/proba-animations/memoryless/memoryless.html)**

  This page shows the memoryless property of $X \sim \mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$, $P(X \ge s + t \mid X \ge s) = P(X \ge t)$.
  After waiting $s$, the density of the remaining time is the density of $X$ again, whatever $s$.
  Press play to let $s$ grow, or drag $s$ and $t$ along the axes.
  A toggle replaces the densities with the survival functions, $S_X(x) = P(X \ge x)$, so that probabilities become heights.
  For comparison, switch to the Weibull distribution, in the form $P(X \ge x) = e^{-\lambda x^\gamma}$.
  It has less time left for $\gamma > 1$ (wear) and more for $\gamma < 1$ (survival of the fittest).
  Another toggle shows the hazard rate, which is flat for the exponential distribution because it does not age.
  Add `#exp` or `#weibull` to the URL to choose the starting distribution.

- **[Memoryless property: raindrops](https://marcevrard.github.io/proba-animations/raindrops/raindrops.html)**

  This page shows the same property with a simulated shower.
  Each tile of a pavement gets drops at rate $\lambda$, so its wait for the first drop follows $\mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$.
  The time already waited, $s$, changes how many tiles are still dry, but not the law of their remaining wait.
  A table compares the tiles still dry at $s$ with all tiles: the same share gets its drop within one second, and the mean wait is $1/\lambda$ for both.
  Three views show it: the histograms of the waits, the share still dry $t$ later, and the mean wait against $s$.
  The number of tiles ranges from 4 to 100,000 (the default).
  With few tiles, the statistics are noisy.
  As the number grows, the mean wait against $s$ flattens to $1/\lambda$.
  The pavement shows every tile up to $40 \times 40$, and a $40 \times 40$ part beyond.

## Using the pages

- In the PDF and CDF page, drag the dots on the axes.

- In the universality animation, play, pause, or drag the slider.
  The space bar also plays or pauses.

- In the memoryless page, press play or drag the dots on the axes.

- In the raindrops page, move the slider for $s$ and choose the number of tiles.
  Press play to watch the shower in real time, or draw a new one.

- The pages work offline.
  Download or clone the repo and open `index.html` in a browser.
  Keep the folders together, since every page loads the shared `plotly.min.js` from the root.

## Layout

```text
index.html                   landing page, one section per animation
plotly.min.js                Plotly library shared by all pages
<animation>/<page>.html      one folder per animation
```

## How the pages are made

This repo only holds the published pages.
Each page is generated by a Python script, with Plotly, from the course materials, which are kept elsewhere.
Please do not edit the HTML files by hand, since the next update overwrites them.
To report a problem or suggest an improvement, open an issue.
